1. Read the error messages thoroughly

Few habits pay off as consistently as actually reading your error messages. They’re often written by the people who built the tools you’re using — people trying to help you debug faster. Resist the reflex to panic and copy-paste the message into a search bar. Instead, slow down, decipher its meaning, and you’ll usually find the exact hint you need to fix the root cause.

2. Keep it simple

Every scalable system began as a small, working prototype. Overengineering early on is one of the easiest traps to fall into. Start simple, make it work, and only then evolve it. Lean designs are easier to test, deploy, and replace when needed. Complexity should be added consciously, not by accident.

3. Stay curious — it’s your superpower

Debugging can be maddening, but perseverance separates the seasoned engineers from the rest. Break problems into chunks, test your assumptions, and keep iterating. It’s astonishing how often the solution appears right after you almost quit. Tenacity builds confidence and deepens technical intuition.
